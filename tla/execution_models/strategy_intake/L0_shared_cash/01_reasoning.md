# Ownership and fault boundaries

Each queue starts with a buy. Its second descriptor repeats the identical buy, cancels its own order, names the other member's original order, or carries the wrong logical ID. The immutable queue contents and which member occupies the first configured slot vary across initial states. Both requests use the same instrument, quote asset and current writer epoch. Each buy reserves one unit including its configured fee bound. Cancel admissions reserve zero units. Native success is an ACK. An open order retains its hold after that ACK.

Only the account process mutates the shared account and the two pending slots. A successful admission, its account WAL sync and the local submission callback form one turn. A later transport poll may reject before the first byte or accept a complete short request. A decoded ACK is another turn. The ghost send count never controls admission or first-write eligibility. An actual stored status on an exact retry bypasses both pending assignments. The original producing queue is recorded independently from reply routing. A nonmember descriptor with a valid policy withdrawal removes its slot. A known order owned by a different logical strategy fails account management authorization even with a perfectly valid original order ID.

The restart configuration reopens a previously settled owner or one that fully sent a buy before ACK. It retains the account WAL, the file descriptors and economic holds. It resets volatile pending state and reopens both queues at the configured first sequence, here one. The successful requalification branch assumes all sent buys remain open, zero fills, matching locks, a higher approved writer and valid private/REST evidence. Old descriptors can retrieve a durable prior result but cannot introduce a fresh old-writer command. The crash model stops before any cancellation reached the venue. The bounded configuration leaves the histories of pending cancellation, intra-WAL crashes, partial TLS writes and expired descriptors to other levels. Cancels targeting locally aborted orders are also outside the input profile.
